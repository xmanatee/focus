Start with one evening window

A digital detox does not need to mean giving up your phone. Choose a repeatable period when you want fewer distractions, such as 20:00-22:00, and decide what you want to do with that time: read, cook, walk, or spend time with someone.

  1. Choose the distractions. Select the feeds, games, or video apps you tend to open without a plan. Keep calls, messages you need, maps, and other essentials available.
  2. Create the schedule. Add a Focus Blocks rule, select blocking during a schedule, and choose the evening hours and days you want it to repeat.
  3. Include browser alternatives. If you normally switch from an app to its website, include that website in your selection as well.
  4. Check a short block first. With Screen Time permission granted and the block enabled, open a selected app during the scheduled period to confirm that protection applies.

New to the app? The iPhone app-blocking guide walks through permission, selection, and the different rule types.

Choose a weekend break you can keep

For a first weekend rule, try one defined window, such as Saturday 10:00-13:00, instead of blocking every non-essential app all day. Use a separate rule if your weekend selection or hours differ from your weekday plan.

If you want a little access spread through the day, a daily app budget may fit better than a fixed window. A schedule reserves specific time away from apps; a budget limits the total allowance.

Review the plan before making it stricter

After trying the schedule, check whether you blocked an app you needed or left an easy distraction available. Adjust the selection and hours deliberately. Add Lock-in only after checking the rule, and choose a setup window when you can make future changes.

Using an iPad as well?

Rules can sync through your own iCloud account, but Apple's private app selections belong to each device. Confirm the apps and enable the rule on each device you want included in the break. See the setup checklist if a synced rule is not blocking.

Private, local, self-directed

Focus Blocks is for your own devices. It does not monitor other people, collect analytics, or require an account.
